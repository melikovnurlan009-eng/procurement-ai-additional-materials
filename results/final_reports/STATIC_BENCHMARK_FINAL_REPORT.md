# Standalone Static Benchmark — Final Report

Scope: `evaluation/final_retrieval_benchmark/` — 60 scenarios (40 dev/20 test), 6 static
retrieval configurations, LLM-judged pooled candidate pool. This is a self-contained pipeline,
separate from the `procurement_research_workbench_v1` matched-DEV/TEST experiment reported in
`MATCHED_DEV_FINAL_REPORT.md`. All numbers below are read directly from
`evaluation/final_retrieval_benchmark/metrics/*.json(l)` and `figures/`, generated by
`compute_metrics.py`, `strict_target_recall.py`, `candidate_ceiling.py`, `error_analysis.py`,
`make_figures.py`, `build_final_tables.py` — no new retrieval or judging was run to produce this
report; only already-finished artifacts were read and aggregated.

## 1. Judging completion (verified from source)

- Total pooled candidates: **5,895**. Successfully judged: **5,895 (100.0%)**. Failed/skipped: **0**.
- All 60 scenarios have complete top-k judgments; no scenario excluded from any metric below.
- Relevance grade distribution across all 5,895 judgments: grade 0 = 1,671; grade 1 = 2,124;
  grade 2 = 1,338; grade 3 = 636; abstained (null) = 126.
- Annotation status: all 5,895 rows are `LLM_FIRST_PASS` (this standalone pipeline's judge did
  not hit the requirement-ID-regeneration bug found and fixed in the workbench's judge contracts
  — its own, simpler single-pass validation never crashed across this full run).

## 2. Main finding

**Candidate generation, not ranking, is this benchmark's dominant bottleneck.** The
candidate-generation-ceiling diagnostic (scenario-level, gold union across all mandatory
requirements) classifies all 60 scenarios. **This table supersedes an earlier version produced
by `candidate_ceiling.py`, which contained a classification-order bug** (it checked "absent from
the lexical+dense union" before checking "present in the final top-10", so a scenario whose gold
was retrieved only via the graph-expansion channel — present in the final result but not in the
narrower lexical+dense union — was misclassified as a candidate-generation failure rather than a
success). The corrected classification (`candidate_ceiling_CORRECTED.py`, same final-top-10
definition, checks success first) is used below; see `provenance/CHANGELOG_FINALIZATION.md` for
the old-vs-corrected numbers and the exact bug.

| Diagnosis | n | % |
|---|---:|---:|
| OK_FOUND_IN_FINAL_TOP10 | 24 | 40.0% |
| CANDIDATE_GENERATION_PROBLEM (gold absent from the lexical+dense union top-50 candidate pool) | 31 | 51.7% |
| RANKING_PROBLEM (gold was retrieved into that union pool but not into the final top-10) | 5 | 8.3% |
| **Total** | **60** | |

Just over half of scenarios fail because the retrieval systems never surface the gold provision
as a lexical/dense *candidate* at all; a further 40% do reach the final top-10 (many via the
graph-expansion channel, which this classification does not treat as part of the "candidate
pool" baseline — see the corrected script's docstring for the exact definitions used). This
remains directionally corroborated by the strict, judge-free `strict_target_recall.py` metric
(recall against corpus-verified essential-evidence chunk_ids only, zero LLM involvement): even
the best config's `strict_recall_10` is only 0.264 (§4) — candidate generation is a real,
substantial limitation, just not as large a share of scenarios as the uncorrected table implied.
Consistent in direction with the TEST-split error classification (`error_analysis.py`): 5 of 7
TEST failures for the production two-lane config are classified `MISSING_CORPUS_EVIDENCE`
(candidate-generation problem), 1 `VOCABULARY_MISMATCH`, 1 `OTHER`. `error_analysis.py` does
read `candidate_ceiling.jsonl`'s `diagnosis` field directly, so it is not independent of the bug
above in general -- but checked directly, the 4 scenarios whose diagnosis actually flipped
(DEV012, DEV027, DEV038, TEST016) do not overlap with the 7 scenarios `error_analysis.py`
classified (TEST006, TEST007, TEST009, TEST013, TEST014, TEST015, TEST020), so its specific
reported numbers above are unaffected by this bug. This was verified directly, not assumed.

## 3. Strongest configuration — a genuine trade-off, not a single winner

| Config | req_coverage_10 | nDCG@10 | regime_error_rate | authority_top1_ok | strict_recall_10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A_lexical | 0.550 | 0.721 | 0.150 | 0.300 | 0.050 |
| B_dense | 0.708 | **0.843** | **0.517** | 0.750 | 0.108 |
| C_hybrid | 0.778 | 0.815 | 0.417 | 0.800 | 0.125 |
| D_hybrid_priors | 0.761 | 0.833 | 0.167 | 0.667 | 0.214 |
| E_hybrid_graph | 0.747 | 0.780 | 0.383 | 0.917 | 0.139 |
| **F_two_lane** | **0.769** | 0.810 | 0.167 | 0.367 | **0.264** |

Full table: `FINAL_RESULTS_TABLE.csv`.

- **`B_dense` has the single highest pooled nDCG@10 (0.843) but also by far the worst
  `regime_error_rate` (0.517) — over half its top-10 results include a grade>=2 passage the
  judge itself flagged as not currently applicable (e.g. a superseded PCR2015 provision for a
  PA2023-only scenario).** A high nDCG number for `B_dense` alone would overstate its practical
  value; this is reported together, not separately.
- `F_two_lane` (the production two-lane legal-aware config actually used by the workbench) and
  `D_hybrid_priors` both combine strong coverage with the lowest regime-error rate (0.167) of any
  config, and `F_two_lane` has the best strict, judge-independent recall (0.264) of all six.
  **This, not raw nDCG, is why `F_two_lane`/`D_hybrid_priors` are the strongest configurations
  overall** — coverage and ranking quality alone are not sufficient; regime correctness matters
  for a legal-research tool.
- **Paired sign test on `req_coverage_10`** (`PAIRWISE_STATISTICS.csv`, two-sided exact sign
  test, alpha=0.05, n=60 scenario pairs per comparison, 2000-sample bootstrap for the 95% CI, no
  multiple-comparison correction applied to any of these figures): `A_lexical` is
  **significantly worse than four of the five** other configs -- `C_hybrid` (p=0.00049),
  `D_hybrid_priors` (p=0.0004), `E_hybrid_graph` (p=0.0004), `F_two_lane` (p=0.00149). **The
  comparison with `B_dense` is borderline and does not cross the 0.05 threshold** (p=0.05224,
  delta=-0.158, 8 wins/33 ties/19 losses) -- this is reported as a non-significant, not a
  significant, result, correcting an earlier draft of this report that stated lexical was
  significantly worse than *every* configuration. Among `C_hybrid`, `D_hybrid_priors`,
  `E_hybrid_graph`, `F_two_lane`, **no pairwise difference is statistically significant** (all
  p >= 0.375, most >= 0.58) — at n=60 these four are not distinguishable from each other on
  requirement coverage alone. This is reported as a genuine null result, not smoothed into a
  false ranking. Exact p-values for all 15 pairwise comparisons, with test/sidedness/alpha/n
  stated identically, are in `FINAL_RESULTS_TABLE.csv`'s companion `PAIRWISE_STATISTICS.csv`,
  regenerated by `build_final_tables.py` (deterministic given a fixed bootstrap seed of 1234).

## 4. Strict verified-target retrieval vs. pooled graded passage relevance — kept separate

These answer different questions and are not interchangeable:

- **Strict verified-target retrieval** (`strict_target_recall.py`): recall against only the
  corpus-verified essential-evidence chunk_id(s) per requirement, zero LLM judgment. This is a
  strict lower bound — acceptable alternatives and supporting-only evidence never count. Best
  config `F_two_lane`: recall@10 = 0.264, scenario-complete@10 = 0.133, MRR = 0.190.
- **Pooled graded passage relevance** (`compute_metrics.py`, `qrels_provisional.jsonl`): any
  judge-graded passage counts, including passages never predicted as gold. Best-by-metric config
  varies (`B_dense` nDCG@10=0.843; `C_hybrid` req_coverage_10=0.778).

The strict metric is uniformly and substantially lower than the pooled metric for every config
(e.g. `F_two_lane`: 0.264 strict vs 0.769 pooled req_coverage_10) — this gap is itself informative:
most of what pooled judging credits as "coverage" is evidence the judge accepted as sufficient
that was never in the small, hand-verified essential-evidence set, i.e. legitimate alternative
evidence, not an error in either metric. Neither metric is treated as the other's replacement or
upper/lower bound proof; both are reported side by side (`FINAL_RESULTS_TABLE.csv`).

## 6. Candidate-generation vs. ranking failures

Already covered in full in §2 (corrected classification). Restated as the headline split:
**51.7% candidate-generation failures vs. 8.3% pure ranking failures vs. 40.0% success** — any
future work aimed at improving this benchmark's numbers should still prioritise query
formulation / candidate recall over re-ranking, since re-ranking cannot fix a document the
retriever never fetched into its lexical/dense candidate pool, though the margin over ranking
failures is smaller than an earlier, buggy version of this analysis suggested.

## 7. Graph wins/losses

Isolating the graph-expansion component (`C_hybrid` = hybrid without the forced-legislation-graph
patch, vs `E_hybrid_graph` = hybrid + graph, same everything else): delta = +0.031 favouring
`C_hybrid` (i.e. graph slightly worse on average), **not statistically significant** (wins 4,
ties 55, losses 1, p=0.375, 95% CI includes zero). **Graph expansion shows no measurable
requirement-coverage benefit on this benchmark** — the two configs disagree on only 5 of 60
scenarios in either direction. This is a genuine negative/inconclusive finding, retained as such
rather than omitted. (Graph's `authority_top1_ok` rate is numerically higher, 0.917 vs 0.800 for
plain hybrid, and its `regime_error_rate` is numerically lower, 0.383 vs 0.417 — small, unproven
secondary signals, not claimed as significant given the above null result on the primary metric.)

## 8. Authority/regime findings

- `authority_top1_ok` (whether the top-1 result is binding when the scenario's gold role demands
  binding evidence, else vacuously true) ranges from 0.300 (`A_lexical`) to 0.917
  (`E_hybrid_graph`). This metric is constructed so that scenarios not requiring binding evidence
  count as automatically "ok" for every config, which inflates and compresses the comparison
  across configs that differ mainly on non-authority-suite scenarios — treat it as directional,
  not a precise measurement of binding-authority accuracy.
- `regime_error_rate` (fraction of scenarios where a wrong-regime, judge-graded>=2 passage reached
  the top-10) is the more reliable of the two regime-related diagnostics here, and is the basis
  for §3's "strongest configuration" conclusion: `B_dense` (0.517) and `C_hybrid` (0.417) are
  markedly worse than `A_lexical`/`D_hybrid_priors`/`F_two_lane` (0.150-0.167). Semantic/dense
  retrieval methods, unconstrained by lexical exactness, retrieve superseded-regime content more
  often — a real, practically important caveat for any legal-research system that would otherwise
  be tempted to lead with a dense-only or dense-heavy configuration purely on nDCG grounds.

## 9. Claims this section supports for the thesis (statistically supported only)

1. Across all 6 static configurations, candidate generation (not ranking) is the dominant failure
   mode, accounting for 51.7% of scenarios where the gold provision is not found in the final
   top-10 (corrected classification; 8.3% are pure ranking failures, 40.0% succeed).
2. `A_lexical` is statistically significantly worse than four of the five other configurations on
   requirement coverage under a two-sided paired sign test at alpha=0.05 (`C_hybrid` p=0.00049,
   `D_hybrid_priors` p=0.0004, `E_hybrid_graph` p=0.0004, `F_two_lane` p=0.00149); the comparison
   with `B_dense` is numerically in the same direction but does not cross the 0.05 threshold
   (p=0.05224) and is reported as not significant.
3. Among `C_hybrid`, `D_hybrid_priors`, `E_hybrid_graph` and `F_two_lane`, no pairwise difference
   in requirement coverage is statistically significant at n=60 -- these four configurations are
   not distinguishable from each other on this metric alone.
4. Graph expansion (`E_hybrid_graph` vs `C_hybrid`) shows no statistically significant
   requirement-coverage benefit on this benchmark (p=0.375).
5. `B_dense`'s highest-of-all-configs nDCG@10 (0.843) co-occurs with by far the worst
   regime-error rate (0.517) -- a real trade-off, not a reason to prefer dense retrieval alone.
6. Strict, judge-independent recall against corpus-verified targets tops out at 0.264
   (`F_two_lane`) -- substantially lower than any pooled/LLM-judged coverage number, confirming
   the retrieval-recall ceiling is a real, structural limitation independent of judge behaviour.

## Negative / inconclusive findings retained (not omitted)

- No statistically significant winner among the four strongest configurations.
- Graph expansion's apparent small `authority_top1_ok`/`regime_error_rate` improvements are not
  backed by a significant primary-metric result and should not be cited as a graph benefit.
- `compound` and `authority` suite estimates (n=4 each) are descriptive only, not stable.
- `error_analysis.py`'s automatic failure classification (`MISSING_CORPUS_EVIDENCE` etc.) is a
  first-pass heuristic over 7 TEST-split cases and is explicitly documented in that script's own
  docstring as needing spot-checking, not ground truth -- see `MANUAL_EVALUATION_AUDIT.md` for
  the cases actually spot-checked.
